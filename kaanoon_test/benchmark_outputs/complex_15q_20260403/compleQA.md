## Q1 — CRIMINAL LAW + EVIDENCE + CONSTITUTIONAL LAW
### *The Custodial Confession & Narco-Analysis Case*

A man ("accused") is arrested in connection with a series of bank robberies. During interrogation:
- Police conduct **narco-analysis and brain mapping** without the accused's written consent, claiming "implied consent" from his lawyer's initial non-objection
- A **confessional statement** is recorded before a magistrate under Section 164 CrPC — however, the accused later claims he was brought directly from police custody with no gap period
- His mobile phone is **seized and mirrored** without a search warrant, using evidence from the mirror copy to track co-accused

At trial:
- The prosecution relies on: the narco-analysis report, the Section 164 statement, WhatsApp messages extracted from the phone mirror, and a **test identification parade** conducted 60 days after arrest
- The accused challenges all four pieces of evidence
- The trial court admits all evidence; the accused appeals

The High Court must consider:
- Whether narco-analysis violates **Article 20(3)** and Selvi v. State of Karnataka
- Whether the Section 164 statement was recorded in compliance with procedural safeguards
- Whether warrantless digital evidence is admissible under the **Indian Evidence Act / BSA**
- Whether the delayed TI parade affects evidentiary value

**Question:** Analyse the **admissibility of each piece of evidence**, the constitutional protections available to the accused, and whether the High Court should uphold the conviction.

1. Does narco-analysis violate the right against self-incrimination under Article 20(3)?
2. What procedural safeguards govern a Section 164 statement and when is it rendered inadmissible?
3. Is evidence obtained from a warrantless phone seizure and mirroring admissible under current Indian law?
4. What is the legal standard for a valid Test Identification Parade and how does delay affect its probative value?
5. Can a conviction be sustained if multiple pieces of key evidence are found inadmissible?

## Q2 — FAMILY LAW + CONSTITUTIONAL LAW + SUCCESSION
### *The Triple Talaq, Maintenance & Inheritance Dispute*

A Muslim woman ("wife") was married in 2015. In 2022, her husband pronounces **triple talaq via WhatsApp voice message**. Thereafter:
- He refuses to pay **iddat maintenance**, claiming the marriage is dissolved
- The wife files a complaint under the **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019**
- The husband argues the Act is unconstitutional as it criminalises a religious practice protected under **Article 25**
- The wife simultaneously files for **maintenance under Section 125 CrPC** (now BNSS) claiming she cannot maintain herself
- The husband argues Section 125 doesn't apply since she is governed by **Muslim Personal Law**

Two years later, the husband dies intestate. His family claims the wife has no inheritance rights since the talaq dissolved the marriage. She challenges this, arguing the talaq was void ab initio under the 2019 Act.

**Question:** Decide the **validity of the talaq, maintenance entitlement, constitutional challenge to the 2019 Act, and inheritance rights**.

1. Is triple talaq via WhatsApp a valid pronouncement under the 2019 Act and does the Act's criminal sanction violate Article 25?
2. Is a Muslim divorced woman entitled to maintenance under Section 125 CrPC / BNSS and how does Shah Bano interact with the Muslim Women Act 1986 and 2019?
3. If the talaq is void ab initio under the 2019 Act, what is the wife's marital status and consequent inheritance rights?
4. How do courts balance **religious personal law versus constitutional guarantees** of equality and dignity?
5. Can the wife simultaneously pursue criminal proceedings under the 2019 Act and civil maintenance under BNSS?

- The state government issues a **closure notice**, which the factory challenges in the NGT
- The factory argues: it holds a valid EC, it complied with conditions at time of grant, and any contamination was caused by **other industrial units** in the area

A PIL is filed before the High Court seeking:
- Application of the **Absolute Liability principle** (M.C. Mehta v. Union of India)
- Compensation under the **Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991**
- Cancellation of the environmental clearance

**Question:** Apply environmental law principles, tortious liability, and constitutional environmental rights to decide the claims.

1. How does **Absolute Liability** differ from Rylands v. Fletcher's Strict Liability and does it apply here even if the factory holds a valid EC?
2. Can an environmental clearance insulate a company from liability once it is proven to cause harm exceeding prescribed limits?
3. What are the procedural and substantive powers of the **NGT** versus the **High Court** in environmental matters — can both operate simultaneously?
4. Does contamination of groundwater violate **Article 21** and what remedies are available to residents?
5. How is causation established when multiple industrial units may contribute to the same environmental damage?

4. Does the **write-off of public bank funds** without parliamentary oversight violate Article 14 or any statutory provision, and does a PIL lie against RBI's regulatory inaction?

2. What are the **transfer pricing rules** under Sections 92-92F of the Income Tax Act and does lack of economic substance in a DTAA-country entity automatically lead to disallowance?
3. Is the **GAAR (General Anti-Avoidance Rule)** applicable to treaty shopping through Mauritius and can it override a DTAA's explicit benefits?
4. What is the binding effect of an **AAR ruling** on the Revenue and the assessee — can it be challenged on grounds of material non-disclosure and what is the procedure?
5. Can **constitutional rights** (Article 265 — tax only by authority of law) be invoked against a transfer pricing adjustment that is allegedly based on subjective arm's length price determination?

## Q15 — INTERNATIONAL LAW + EXTRADITION + FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS

**Question:** Examine FEO Act constitutionality, extradition law principles, and the rights of third parties in attachment proceedings.

3. What is the **dual criminality principle** in extradition law — if two of the 12 charges don't correspond to offences in Country X, can extradition proceed for the remaining charges?
4. Can Indian courts attach assets of **family members** not named in the FEO petition — what standard of proof is required to pierce the family asset veil?
5. Does **retrospective application** of the FEO Act to transactions preceding its enactment violate Article 20(1) (protection against ex post facto law) — and does civil forfeiture attract the same protection as criminal punishment?

## 🧪 HOW TO USE THESE 15 QUESTIONS

Each question is engineered to test an AI legal system on:

**Reasoning depth** — Can it identify all the legal issues embedded in the facts, not just the obvious ones?

**Statutory accuracy** — Does it cite correct sections, current law, and distinguish between repealed and live statutes (e.g., IPC vs BNS, CrPC vs BNSS)?

**Jurisdiction logic** — Does it correctly identify which forum has jurisdiction, where conflicts exist, and how concurrent proceedings interact?

**Constitutional balancing** — Does it apply proportionality, the rights-restriction framework, and landmark precedents correctly?

**Procedural intelligence** — Does it spot natural justice violations, limitation issues, and locus standi problems?

**Method:** Feed each scenario to any AI legal assistant. Ask it to generate 5 clarifying questions before answering. Then give fact-consistent answers to those questions and evaluate whether the final legal analysis is accurate, nuanced, and procedurally sound.
