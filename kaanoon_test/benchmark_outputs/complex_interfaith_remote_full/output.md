## SYNTHESIS – WHY THE COURT SHOULD UPHOLD THE WOMAN’S AUTONOMY AND THE VALIDITY OF THE SMA MARRIAGE  

1. **Constitutional primacy of adult autonomy** – Article 21 (and Article 25) protect the woman’s *right to choose* her partner and religion. The *magistrate’s* record of a *voluntary* declaration is *prima facie* evidence of free will; the State bears the *onus* to *disprove* it with *positive* proof of *force* or *fraud*.

2. **Special Marriage Act is a secular, protective statute** – The SMA marriage remains *valid* until *annulled* on the basis of *fraud* *existing at the time of solemnisation* (Sec. 12). The later conversion and second personal‑law ceremony do not satisfy this requirement; at most they give rise to *bigamy* liability (IPC 494) but do **not** invalidate the SMA marriage.

3. **Forced‑conversion law demands concrete coercion** – Karnataka’s anti‑conversion law defines *force* and *allurement* narrowly. The WhatsApp chats show *emotional* confusion, not *physical* or *monetary* coercion. Hence, the statutory threshold for a cognizable offence is not met; any inquiry must be *targeted* and *evidence‑based*.

4. **Police power is circumscribed by reasonable suspicion** – The woman’s voluntary court appearance defeats the *illegal detention* element required for a habeas corpus claim and limits the police’s authority to detain B. Any further detention must satisfy the *reasonable suspicion* test (Lalita Kumari) and be *proportionate* (Maneka Gandhi).

5. **Burden of proof rests on the complainant** – In criminal matters the standard is *beyond reasonable doubt*; in matrimonial challenges it is *pre‑ponderance*. The *WhatsApp* evidence, without corroboration, cannot overturn the *in‑court* declaration of free consent.

6. **‘Love‑jihad’ is a non‑statutory label** – The court must ignore the label and adjudicate only on *statutory* offences. Political rhetoric cannot create a *new* cause of action (Shambhu Kharwar).

7. **State must act proportionately** – While the State must investigate any *genuine* allegation of coercion, it cannot allow *media* or *political* pressure to erode the woman’s constitutional rights. Protective measures (e.g., police protection) may be ordered, but any *restriction* on liberty must be the *least intrusive* means.

---

### CONCLUSION  

The balance of probabilities, the *high* evidentiary threshold for coercion, and the *protective* purpose of the Special Marriage Act compel the Court to **affirm**:

* the **validity** of the SMA marriage;  
* the **voluntary** nature of the woman’s conversion and second ceremony;  
* the **absence** of a cognizable offence under the anti‑conversion law; and  
* the **unlawfulness** of the habeas corpus petition and any further detention of the husband.

Accordingly, the Court should **dismiss** the criminal complaints for lack of *prima facie* evidence, **reject** the claim that the SMA marriage is void, **order** only a *limited* inquiry into the conversion (if any) and, if necessary, **grant** police protection to the woman, thereby upholding **Article 21** autonomy while satisfying the State’s duty to prevent genuine coercion.
